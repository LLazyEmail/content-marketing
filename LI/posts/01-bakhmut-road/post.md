# The narrowing road into Bakhmut

The logistics route into Bakhmut was narrowing to a single, critical artery—one road that every supply, drop-off, and casualty evacuation depended on.

Russian strategy at the time was straightforward and relentless: total interdiction. They saturated the sky with night reconnaissance and strike drones, keeping constant eyes on that thin stretch of road and particularly the choke point beneath the bridge. Within just five to seven days, our battalion alone lost eight or nine vehicles along that passage.

Yet, the work didn't stop. Every single day, we drove two of our own cars back and forth down that exact strip to reach our team.

Standing there, watching the count of burned-out chassis along the shoulder grow daily, was surreal. You knew the drones were watching the bridge. You knew the risk profile for every run. And still, you knew with absolute certainty that your people would drive it today, tomorrow, and the day after that—because there was simply no alternative.

Before the assault shifted the front, that area was just a regular point on the map. It was a place we passed through on routine errands to pick up construction supplies and tools for ongoing work. Today, that civilian context is gone, and the territory itself is occupied.

What remains is the stark memory of what it took to keep a force sustained under absolute surveillance—and the quiet, routine courage of the drivers who stepped into those vehicles every single day.
